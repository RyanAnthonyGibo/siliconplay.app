About SiliconPlay

Who makes the app, and what the app actually is

SiliconPlay is a free Android emulator for Mac computers with Apple silicon. I made it so a game or an APK can run on the Mac’s own ARM cores, with graphics drawn by Metal, without a kernel extension and without a background service left behind when you quit. The current version is 1.0.0. This page is the plain account of who operates it and what I have and have not tested. The blog is where the step-by-step guides live.

What the app runs

Supported Macs are M1–M6, including Pro, Max, and Ultra. I have tested SiliconPlay on macOS Tahoe and macOS 27 Golden Gate. I have not certified other macOS versions. If you try one, I want the report, with the version and the chip, more than I want to guess.

Android 16 runs natively on the Mac’s ARM cores through Apple’s Hypervisor framework. The DMG does not contain the emulator or the system image. The first launch asks you to accept the Android SDK license and downloads Google’s command-line tools, the Apple silicon emulator, platform-tools, and the Play arm64 system image into a folder you choose. You can point the app at an SDK you already have if that folder contains emulator, adb, and sdkmanager. The tablet it creates is landscape, 1920×1080 at 320 dpi, auto-rotate off, and named Android 16 Tablet.

Google Play on that tablet is the store from the Play image, not a store I bundled. SiliconPlay never sees the password you type there. Vulkan is drawn by Metal. Shaders are compiled in safe math mode so foliage renders correctly. On a MacBook Pro with M3 Max I measured up to 60 fps in a demanding 3D game, at render resolutions up to 2560×1440, with 7–43 ms from key to game. Results vary by game, Mac, and settings. I have not published per-chip benchmarks, and I will not invent them.

What I left out on purpose

There is no kernel extension to approve and no background service to uninstall. Delete the app and the app is gone. The SDK folder stays until you delete it, because that download is Google’s emulator and system image, not a hidden driver. SiliconPlay’s own files are under ~/Library/Application Support/SiliconPlay.

Root is optional and off by default. The Play image refuses adb root, so a rooted tablet is a second instance on the Google APIs image, and that image does not include Play Store. The app does not modify games. Follow each game’s terms if you sign in with an account you care about.

Direct touch is the default. A keymap, including the generic MMORPG (WASD) layout and any community preset the app offers, stays unloaded until you accept it. Command-I imports an APK and installs it. Split APKs and XAPK archives are not installed by this version. A package with no ARM64 build will not run. The system image does not mount a host folder as a live disk.

An anonymous usage counter uses TelemetryDeck. It does not include personal data and it does not include advertising identifiers. The privacy notice is the place for that, for the website, and for how ads on this site work. This page is not a second privacy policy.
