Looking for a BlueStacks Alternative on Mac? Try SiliconPlay

By Ryan Gibo · 26 October 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026

BlueStacks Air brought Android gaming to Apple Silicon, and for many people it works well. But it isn’t the only option anymore. SiliconPlay is a free Android emulator made by an independent developer specifically for Apple Silicon Macs, from M1 through M6 chips. Here is an honest look at when it’s a good fit.

What’s different

SiliconPlay runs Android 16 (BlueStacks Air runs Android 13, per its own requirements as of September 2026). It uses Apple’s Hypervisor framework and Metal directly, installs no kernel extensions or background services, and offers optional root for developers — something gaming-focused emulators usually don’t.

Where BlueStacks may still win

BlueStacks has years of game-specific presets, multi-instance and a large support team. If you depend on those, it may still suit you. SiliconPlay is newer and built by one person, so feedback directly shapes it.

What I can say without borrowing their product

BlueStacks Air, as of its own requirements in September 2026, runs Android 13 on Apple silicon and is aimed at games. It has years of game-specific presets, it can run more than one instance, and it has a large support team. SiliconPlay runs Android 16 on the Mac’s ARM cores through Apple’s Hypervisor framework, draws Vulkan with Metal, and compiles shaders in safe math mode so foliage renders correctly. I am one person. If you depend on a preset library and a support desk, BlueStacks may still suit you. I would rather say that than pretend a younger app has the same shelf of profiles.

SiliconPlay does not install a kernel extension or a background service, and it does not bundle extra software. Optional root is off by default, and it is a second tablet on the Google APIs image because the Play image refuses adb root. That rooted tablet does not include Play Store. Gaming-focused emulators usually do not offer that split, because they are not trying to be an adb-root test bench. If you need it, it is there. If you do not, leave it off and use the Play tablet.

NoxPlayer, LDPlayer, and MEmu do not run on Apple silicon. I am not going to explain that by naming an architecture this site does not discuss. They are not options on the Macs SiliconPlay supports. UTM is free and has no 3D acceleration, so it is not for these games. The Android Studio emulator is the official one and the right tool when you want the IDE. It is heavy because of that IDE. The lineup with the job each one is good at is on the comparison page.

A concrete way to try SiliconPlay beside it

You can keep BlueStacks installed. SiliconPlay does not ask you to remove it. The test I care about is whether your game runs on this tablet, with your keys.

  1. Use a Mac with M1–M6, including Pro, Max, and Ultra. I have tested macOS Tahoe and macOS 27 Golden Gate. Other versions: try them and tell me.
  2. Download the DMG, drag SiliconPlay to Applications, and open it.
  3. Let the first run download Android into an SDK folder you choose, or point at an SDK that already has emulator, adb, and sdkmanager. The tablet is Android 16, 1920×1080, 320 dpi, landscape, auto-rotate off.
  4. If the app says there is no JDK, run brew install openjdk@17 and do the package step again. /usr/bin/java on macOS is a stub.
  5. Open Play Store on the tablet and sign in. SiliconPlay never sees that password. Or start the instance and press Command-I to import an APK. A drop works too. Split APKs and XAPK archives are not installed by this version.
  6. Play with direct touch first. If a preset is offered, it stays off until you accept it. Or apply MMORPG (WASD) and move the buttons onto the art.

The only performance numbers I will put next to that test are the ones I measured: a MacBook Pro with M3 Max, a demanding 3D game, up to 60 fps, render resolution up to 2560×1440, 7–43 ms from key to game. Results vary by game, Mac, and settings. I have not published a head-to-head against BlueStacks, so this page does not contain one. Anyone who shows you a chart I did not write is not quoting me.

Controls, because that is where a switch feels different

Command-I imports an APK. F12 or Command-K toggles Typing and Game keys. A focused text field switches to Typing by itself. Command-Shift-G shows or hides the on-screen buttons. Escape leaves fullscreen, and outside fullscreen it is Android Back while the display is focused. Shift-Escape releases every virtual finger. F10 is direct touch. The default keymap, if you apply it, is WASD or the arrows to move, J held to attack, 1–6 for skills, Space to jump, F to interact, M for the map, B for the bag, and the right mouse button to look. A few of those skills also listen for gamepad buttons. The controller presses the same on-screen chips. It is not a second scheme.

The first time SiliconPlay follows the emulator window, macOS asks for Accessibility. Until you allow it, keep the SiliconPlay window focused. BlueStacks has its own key story. I am not going to map their chords onto mine. The list I will stand behind is the shortcut list from version 1.0.0.

What will disappoint you if you wanted a clone

  • There is no large support team. Mail comes to me. The contact page shows the address as text.
  • Presets exist for some games, including ones such as Genshin Impact, Roblox, Call of Duty Mobile, and Clash of Clans, and they stay off until you accept them. A missing preset means direct touch or the generic WASD layout, not a hidden download.
  • Create New Instance is on the main window. That is not the same product as a gaming multi-instance feature with a preset per window. If that workflow is why you stayed with BlueStacks, stay.
  • An app with no ARM64 build will not run. Android 16 on this tablet will not execute a package that never shipped an ARM64 library.

There is no kernel extension to uninstall if you decide it is not for you. Delete the app. The SDK folder remains because it is a download, not a driver. SiliconPlay’s own files are under ~/Library/Application Support/SiliconPlay. More of the first-run detail is in the setup guide.
