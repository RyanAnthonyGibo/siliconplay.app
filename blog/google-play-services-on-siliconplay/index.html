Google Play and Play services on the SiliconPlay tablet

By Ryan Gibo · Updated 12 November 2026

Google Play on SiliconPlay is the store that comes with the Play system image. I did not bundle a store in the DMG, and the Mac app does not get a copy of your password. This page is the part people hit after sign-in: Play services, updates, apps that refuse to start, and the tablet that does not have the store at all.

SiliconPlay is the Mac app I build around the official Android ARM64 emulator. It is for Apple silicon, M1 through M6, including Pro, Max, and Ultra. It is not compatible with Intel Macs. I have tested it on macOS Tahoe and macOS 27 Golden Gate. Other macOS versions are welcome as reports.

Two images, and only one of them has the store

The default tablet is the Android 16 Play image: landscape, 1920×1080, 320 dpi, auto-rotate off, named Android 16 Tablet. That image includes Google Play. Sign in on the Android home screen, inside Play Store. The steps for the password screen itself are in signing in on the tablet. This page starts from “I am signed in, and something still complains.”

The Play image refuses adb root. If you needed root, you created a second instance on the Google APIs image. That image does not include Play Store, and it does not grow Play services because you sideloaded one APK you found in a forum. An app that exits with “Get Google Play services” on the rooted tablet is describing the image you booted, not a SiliconPlay bug. Use the Play tablet for that app. Keep the rooted tablet for the work that actually needed root. The split is root versus the Play image.

What the Mac app receives

Nothing from that sign-in form. The password stays in the guest. I do not want it in email, and I cannot reset it. Account recovery is Google’s. A purchase inside a game is the publisher’s and Google’s. A ban under a game’s terms is the publisher’s. SiliconPlay does not modify games. Optional root, off by default, does not change that.

The website may show ads through Google AdSense. Those ads are not inside the Android window, and they are not the Play Store. The privacy notice is where cookies and third-party ads are described. Do not confuse a cookie on siliconplay.app with a Play session on the tablet.

Updates

Play updates itself, and it updates apps, inside the guest, the way it does on a phone. SiliconPlay does not push those updates through the DMG. If Play says an app needs an update, do that in Play on the Play tablet. If the system image itself is old relative to what a brand-new phone would have, that is a property of the image version 1.0.0 downloaded, which is the Android 16 Play arm64 image. I am not documenting a secret “update the system image” button beyond what the first-run download and sdkmanager already are. If you pointed at your own SDK, you are responsible for what that SDK contains.

A game that worked last month and now says the Play version is too old needs an update from Play, on the Play image, with the instance running. Cmd+I will not update a store app. Cmd+I installs a single APK you already have as a file.

Sideload and the store on the same tablet

You can be signed in and also install a single APK. Those are two doors onto the same Play tablet, not a conflict by themselves. The failure starts when the file is a split set or an XAPK, which this version does not install, or when the APK is a different signature than the store copy and Android refuses to replace it. I am not going to tell you to uninstall a game and lose local data without saying that out loud: if the game stores progress on the device and you are not also signed into a cloud save, replacing the package can drop that progress. I cannot see whether your game syncs. Check the game before you delete it.

An APK with no ARM64 library will not run, Play services or not. The store’s own copy of a current game usually has the right ABI because Play serves a build for the device. That is one reason to prefer the store when the title is simply available there. The decision guide is Play Store or an APK.

When an app says Play services are missing or out of date

  1. Confirm you are on the Play tablet, not the APIs tablet. The window title and the presence of Play Store are the check. If there is no Play Store icon, you are on the wrong instance for this error.
  2. Open Play Store, and let it finish its own update if it offers one. A brand-new image sometimes needs that pass before other apps are happy.
  3. Open the app again. If it sends you to Play, follow that, still inside the guest.
  4. If the app was a sideload of a build that expects a particular services version, the store build of the same app is the cleaner test. I will not tell you to install a random services APK over the image. That is how you get a guest that no longer updates cleanly, and version 1.0.0 does not have a repair button for it.
  5. If Play itself will not stay signed in, go back to the sign-in guide. The usual causes are the wrong image, a guest that never finished first boot, or a Google-side prompt I cannot see. A screenshot of the guest, not of your password, is the useful attachment.

Accounts you care about

A tablet is still a device in your Google account. Signing in adds it to that account the way a phone would. Signing out is something you do in the guest, and removing the device from the Google account is something you do on Google’s side if you want it gone from the device list. Deleting the Mac app does not send that request for you. The SDK folder and the guest data live until you delete them. Uninstall covers the files. It does not log you out of Google.

I use a separate Google account for emulator experiments when I do not want a test device sitting next to my phone in the account’s device list. That is ordinary hygiene, not a SiliconPlay feature. The app will not create the account for you.

Shortcuts while Play is open

Play’s search box is a text field. Click it and the toolbar should switch to Typing. If keys are hitting buttons instead of the search box, you are still in game keys.

FAQ

Does SiliconPlay include the Play Store in the DMG?

No. The first run downloads the Play arm64 system image, unless your existing SDK already supplies what the app needs. The store is on that image.

Can I use Play and root on one tablet?

Not on this Play image. It refuses adb root. Root is the other instance, and that instance does not include the store.

The app says my device is not certified. What can you do?

I am not going to promise a certification workaround. Play’s device checks are Google’s. If the Play image’s own Play Store is signed in and updating apps, start from that fact and tell me the exact sentence on screen if a specific app still refuses. I will not ship a “fix integrity” toggle I do not have.

Is a Play password stored on the Mac?

Not by SiliconPlay. Do not send the password to siliconplay.app@gmail.com. I will not ask for it.
