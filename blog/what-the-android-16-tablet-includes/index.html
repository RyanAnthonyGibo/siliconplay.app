What the Android 16 tablet in SiliconPlay includes, and what it does not

By Ryan Gibo · Updated 12 November 2026

The thing SiliconPlay boots is a specific Android 16 tablet, not “Android” in the abstract and not a copy of your phone. Version 1.0.0 creates it with a short list of defaults. This page is that list, plus the absences that cause most of the surprised mail: no Play on the rooted image, no shared Mac folder, no split-APK installer, no second graphics API hiding in a menu.

SiliconPlay is a Mac app I wrote around the official Android ARM64 emulator. The graphics translation libraries are rewritten for compatibility and frame rate. Vulkan is drawn by Metal, and shaders compile in safe math mode so foliage renders correctly. The app runs on Apple silicon from M1 through M6, including Pro, Max, and Ultra. It is not compatible with Intel Macs. I have tested it on macOS Tahoe and macOS 27 Golden Gate. The tablet does not become a different product on a different chip in that list.

What the first run creates

The DMG does not contain the emulator or the system image. Saying yes to the download accepts the Android SDK license and fetches Google’s command-line tools, the Apple silicon emulator, platform-tools, and the Play arm64 system image into a folder you choose. You can point at an SDK you already have if it contains emulator, adb, and sdkmanager.

The instance is one landscape tablet:

  • Name: Android 16 Tablet.
  • System: Android 16, Play image, ARM64.
  • Panel: 1920×1080.
  • Density: 320 dpi.
  • Orientation: landscape, auto-rotate off.

I did not pick those numbers as advice. They are the defaults this version writes. Auto-rotate off means a game that expects you to flip the phone will not get a rotate from the window being dragged. If a title demands portrait and will not draw in landscape, that is a property of the title plus this default. I am not documenting a rotate button I have not verified, so I will not send you to one. Tell me the title if you hit that wall.

The 1920×1080 panel is not the render resolution of every game. On a MacBook Pro with M3 Max I measured one demanding 3D game at up to 60 fps and render resolutions up to 2560×1440, with 7–43 ms from key to game. Results vary. Do not conflate the panel with that measurement. Performance keeps them apart.

What the Play image includes

Google Play is on this image. It is not a store I packed into the DMG. You open Play Store on the Android home screen and sign in there. The Mac app does not receive the password. Play services are the image’s, updated by Play inside the guest, not by a SiliconPlay updater. Apps that refuse to start without Play services belong on this tablet, not on the rooted one.

The Play image refuses adb root. That is not a bug in your adb binary. Root is optional, off by default, and it is a second instance on the Google APIs image. That second tablet does not include Play Store. You can keep both. You cannot merge them by wishing. The two-tablet explanation is the policy. Play services is what to do when an app asks for them.

What is not in the guest

  • A live mount of a Mac folder. Finder is not a window onto /sdcard. Copy files with adb to /sdcard/SiliconPlay, or install a single APK with Cmd+I.
  • A split-APK or XAPK installer. One APK file is the unit this version installs. An archive with several splits stops at the door on every chip.
  • A translator for a package that shipped no ARM64 library. The guest is ARM64. Most current apps include that library. Older ones sometimes do not, and they will not start.
  • Your phone’s apps, accounts, or saves. Sign in again. Cloud save is the game’s feature, not mine.
  • A kernel extension or a background service on the Mac. Quit the app and the app is gone. The SDK folder remains because it is the download, not a driver.
  • A per-chip Android. M1 through M6 boot the same kind of tablet.

Input, which is part of the tablet even though it lives in the Mac app

Direct touch is the default. The pointer is a finger on that 1920×1080 panel. A keymap is unloaded until you accept one. The generic preset is MMORPG (WASD): WASD or arrows to move, J held to attack, 1–6 for skills, and the same buttons under a gamepad. Click the image or the keys go nowhere useful. A focused text field switches to Typing.

Sensors, calls, and the other phone things people assume

This is a tablet window for apps and games, built the way version 1.0.0 builds it. I am not going to claim a particular sensor suite, a cellular radio, or a camera pass-through. I have not written those as features, so they are not features on this page. If a game asks for a sensor the guest does not expose, the game will do whatever that game does when the sensor is missing. Tell me the title and the sentence it showed if you want that case written down after I have seen it. I will not fill the gap from another emulator’s spec sheet.

Location, accounts, and notifications that do work are the image’s Android behavior once you have signed in, not a Mac menu I can toggle from the desktop. When something prompts inside the guest, deal with it inside the guest. The Mac app’s job is the window, the keymap, and the install path.

More than one tablet

Create New Instance is on the main window even with Pro Mode off. A second instance is a second Android, with its own data. Sync input exists as a named action when more than one is running. It is not a way to mirror your phone. It is not a way to give the rooted image a Play Store. If you only wanted one game, one Play tablet is the whole product.

FAQ

Can I change the 1920×1080 default?

I am not documenting an unverified control. The default is what this version creates. A game’s internal resolution is the game’s setting, and it is a different number.

Does auto-rotate off mean every game is landscape?

It means the tablet does not rotate itself. A game that only draws a portrait UI may letterbox, refuse, or look wrong. That is the combination of this default and that game. Send me the title rather than a request for a secret rotate chord.

Is the Play image a phone or a tablet?

It is the landscape tablet named above. Some phone-only layouts will look like tablet layouts. That is the panel you got.

Where are the Mac-side files?

~/Library/Application Support/SiliconPlay for the app’s own files. The SDK folder is the one you chose. Both are explained in disk space and uninstall.

The setup narrative is the first run. The install rules are Cmd+I and adb.
