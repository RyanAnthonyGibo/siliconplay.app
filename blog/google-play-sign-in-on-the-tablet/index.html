Sign in to Google Play on the Android 16 tablet

By Ryan Gibo · 3 November 2026

Google Play on SiliconPlay is not a store I bundled into the DMG, and it is not a browser tab on the Mac. It is the Play Store app that comes with the Android 16 Play image. You open it on the tablet, you type the password into Android, and SiliconPlay never sees that password. This page is the sign-in, the tablet that does not have a store, and the cases that look like a login failure and are a different problem.

The image has to be the Play image

First run downloads the Play arm64 system image, after you accept the Android SDK license, and creates one landscape tablet named Android 16 Tablet. The default is 1920×1080 at 320 dpi, auto-rotate off. Google Play shows up after that image is in place. If you pointed SiliconPlay at an existing SDK, the tablet it creates is still that Play image, provided the download or the SDK can supply it. The steps up to the home screen are in the first-run guide.

A rooted tablet uses the Google APIs image, because the Play image refuses adb root. Play Store is not included on that tablet. If you are staring at an Android home screen with no store icon, check whether you created the APIs instance. Signing in is not hiding. The store was never on that image. Keep the Play tablet for the account, and use the APIs tablet only when you need adb root. The split is in root and the Play image.

Open the store and sign in

  1. Start the Play instance. The main window’s Start button is that step.
  2. On the Android home screen, open Play Store. Direct touch is the default. The pointer is a finger. You do not need a keymap to tap an icon.
  3. Sign in with the Google account you mean to use. The password goes to Google’s app on the tablet. It is not sent to me, and it is not a field on siliconplay.app.
  4. Install the game or app from the store the way you would on a phone. The tablet is landscape. A store page designed for a portrait phone is still the same store. Scroll it.

If a text field is focused, the toolbar switches to Typing and the keyboard goes to Android as text. That is what you want for an email address and a password. If letters are not appearing, press F12 or Command-K until the toolbar says Typing, and click the field again. Game keys will try to turn those letters into touches. That is the wrong mode for a login box. The three modes are in typing, game keys, and direct touch.

Command-I is still available while you are on the store. It imports an APK and installs it. It does not sign you in, and it does not type into the password field. Use it when you have a file. Use the store when you do not.

What the Mac app does and does not receive

SiliconPlay does not see the Play password. I do not get a copy of your purchases, and the website does not log the account you used on the tablet. The privacy notice is the full list of what the app and the site do handle: an anonymous usage counter through TelemetryDeck that does not include personal data and does not include advertising identifiers, and, if you activate a license, a license key and a hashed machine fingerprint sent to a server we run. Those are not your Play login. Read the privacy notice rather than a summary if you are deciding whether to sign in.

The site may show ads. That is also in the privacy notice, including how consent works where the law requires it. Ads on the website are not the Play Store, and they are not running inside the game window.

Follow each game’s terms. SiliconPlay includes optional root and does not modify games. Root is off by default. Signing into a game on the Play tablet is the ordinary case. I cannot undo an account decision a publisher makes.

Install a file instead of, or as well as, the store

Start the instance, then drop an .apk or press Command-I. adb copies go to /sdcard/SiliconPlay. Split APKs and XAPK archives are not installed by this version. An app with no ARM64 build will not run on this tablet even if Play would have offered a different variant on a phone. Most current apps include an ARM64 build. If Play itself refuses the device, that refusal is Play’s, based on the tablet you are showing it. I have not published a compatibility bypass, and I will not describe one.

The image does not mount a host folder as a live disk. You cannot drop a file into a Finder folder and expect Play, or the files app, to see it. Command-I, a drop onto the instance, or adb are the paths that exist.

When the store will not open or will not stay signed in

  • The instance is stopped. Start it.
  • You are on the APIs tablet. There is no Play Store to open. Start the Play instance.
  • The first-run download did not finish, so the image is not really the Play image. Run the download again rather than signing in against a half-written system.
  • The keyboard is in Game keys while a field is focused. Switch to Typing with F12 or Command-K.
  • You are fullscreen and you wanted the Mac window. Escape leaves fullscreen. Outside fullscreen, Escape is Android Back while the display is focused, which is also how you leave a Play page.
  • A virtual finger is stuck down over the store. Shift-Escape releases every virtual finger.

I have not published a separate “clear Play data” button. If you need the tablet’s app data gone, you are in Android’s own app settings on the tablet, or you are replacing the image by setting up again. I will not invent a menu path inside Play that I have not documented from version 1.0.0.

Shortcuts that show up during sign-in

Shortcuts while the store or a login is on screen
KeysAction
Command-IImport an APK and install it. Does not enter your password.
F12, or Command-KToggle Typing and Game keys. Typing is the one for the login fields.
Command-Shift-GShow or hide the on-screen game buttons, so they are not covering the store.
EscapeLeave fullscreen, or Android Back when not fullscreen and the display is focused.
Shift-EscapeRelease every virtual finger.
F10Switch to direct touch, which is the simple way to tap the store.

Supported Macs are M1 through M6, tested on macOS Tahoe and macOS 27 Golden Gate. Play sign-in does not add a chip to that list and does not remove one. If sign-in works on the tablet and a game then fails to draw, the next page is how graphics are drawn, not a second login.
